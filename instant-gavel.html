Challenge 1: Users losing progress on the submission form

The initial request

Users were losing progress on a complex submission form due to session timeouts. A drafts feature was shipped as a quick fix, based on the fact that users said they filled out the form over several days.

Lowest effort option

The problem persisted. Saving a draft didn't match how people actually moved through the form. The friction wasn't lost data; it was the form's rigid, single-session structure.

The more considered fix

A step-by-step submission dashboard, where each stage saves independently. One long session became a set of discrete steps users could complete on their own schedule, with no risk of losing progress. Post-launch, there were no further reports of lost submissions, and the pattern was later reused for other multi-step forms in the product.

Challenge 2: Negotiating the Auction Agreement

The initial request

No system existed for negotiating Auction Agreement terms before publishing.
The original request was a strict sequential flow:

  1. Client submits auction
  2. Admin sets terms (launch date, bidding window, reserve price, estimated sale price, Instant Gavel price)
  3. Seller is notified
  4. Seller approves (triggers e-signature, countersigned by CEO) or rejects and resubmits

What we found

This matched everyone's first mental model, but it locked negotiation into a rigid back-and-forth. Every round depended on both sides being available, with only approve, reject, or resubmit as options.

The fix

A fully flexible chat, scoped to the auction review. Either side could send documents, share updates, or propose changes at any time, with no formal response step gating the next move.

The result

The client was won over immediately. Post-launch, the chat ended up solving problems well beyond its original scope, like sharing marketing materials.

Crashes reduced by 100%
