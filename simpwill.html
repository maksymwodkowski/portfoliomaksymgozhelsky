Challenge: Visual style concept approval

References

The stakeholder came in with one reference already in mind for the visual direction. To make sure we weren't locking onto a single influence too early, I gathered a wider set of references from other products with strong illustration-based styles, giving us more to align on before committing to a direction.

Concept option 1: Aligning on look and feel

My first instinct was to keep the styling closer to formal, given the sensitivity of the topic. But the business wanted the product to feel as positive as possible, not heavy or clinical. So we went through several concept iterations, each one pushing further from formal and closer to warm and approachable, until we landed on a look and feel both sides were aligned on.

Service Pages

Alongside the homepage, each core offering (Wills, Funerals, Probate & Estate Administration, and Lasting Power of Attorney) got its own dedicated page, giving users a clear, focused entry point into the specific service they came for.

Documents management

User is able to keep all his related documents in one place. Also to keep track on a progress of each one. The key advantage is that at any step it is possible to leave and finalise later, since writing a will is a complex task.

Stepper Dashboard

Will writing process is consuming. So to make it easier to finish and convenient to analyse, it is split into logical steps. And it is possible to go back and edit an already filled one.

Forms

There are many forms, such as “Personal information”, “Add your partner”, “Add a child” and many others.

Guides

A “knowledge base” with a convenient navigation.

Business Goal Considered

After the user completes their primary purchase, we gently introduce a relevant additional service.
